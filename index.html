Where the tools stand

The textbook, the donor profiler, and the strategy tool are all usable right now. The workspace (RaiseMoney.ai itself) is the newest and the most ambitious, and it's the one I most want your eyes on. The rest of this page lays out exactly where each one is.

The tools
How to Raise Money The method, written down Beta live

The whole approach as a field manual you can talk to. Read it straight through, or tell it what you're working on and it reshapes around you — compress a chapter to a page, prep for a donor call, hand it a draft proposal for a read. Everything in it comes from people who've actually raised the money; the AI just helps you use it.

Solid. This is the thinking the other three tools are built on — a good place to start.
Read it →
ProspectAI Read the person Beta live

Give it a name and it does the deep reading for you, then hands back a read on how that person actually decides — what draws them in, what makes them withdraw, the contradiction you can work with. You get a full profile, a one-page briefing, and a guide for the room.

Works well. A full run does real research, so give it a few minutes rather than expecting an instant answer.
Open ProspectAI →
StrategyAI Shape the case Beta live

Bring your organization's materials and it drafts your 1-2-3 — five to choose from. Pick one, sharpen it in conversation, grade it, then build it out into the implementation a proposal can stand on. You can also tune the 1-2-3 for a specific funder — tell it who you're presenting to and it shapes the strategy for that room.

Usable now. Feed it real documents (a deck, a plan, a campaign page) and it gets noticeably sharper.
Open StrategyAI →
RaiseMoney.ai The workspace Alpha

The piece that pulls the rest together. Walk out of a meeting and say what happened in plain language; it files the person, the commitment, the amount, the stage, and the next step — you never touch a field — and tells you what to do next. A working memory and a coaching colleague in one.

The newest and roughest of these, and the one your feedback matters most on. It's invite-only while I build — if you don't have a login yet, ping me on Signal and I'll set you up.
Go to the workspace →
The MCP The whole thing, in your own AI Exploring

An MCP server — the protocol that lets your own AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, whatever you use) reach into an outside system as a native tool. The aim here is the most ambitious version of that: all of RaiseMoney.ai — the capture, the pipeline, the profiler, the strategy work — plus the entire textbook running underneath as the brain, brought to bear on whatever you're actually doing in your own chat. You're working a donor, a proposal, a hard meeting; the whole method and every tool are right there, applied to that, where you already think — instead of bouncing between separate apps.

Earliest stage of the lot — I've worked out how to build it, but it isn't something you can use yet. Listing it because it's where everything's pointed.

Where it's going

Right now these are separate tools at separate addresses. The plan is to bring them into one: you talk through a meeting, and the reading on the person, the strategy for the work, and the record of the relationship all move together — no re-entering anything, no switching tabs.

RaiseMoney.ai is where that's happening. The other three already work on their own; over the next while they fold into the workspace as the parts of one job rather than separate stops. One thing won't change underneath it: the facts stay fixed and code-checked, the AI only ever interprets what a meeting meant — it never invents the meeting, and it never makes the ask for you.

And if you're technical and want to get under the hood, I'm glad to share the git repos so you can see exactly how any of this works. Just reach out.
